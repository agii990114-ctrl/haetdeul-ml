## 1. Bug found before the experiment could be trusted

`train.py:437` fixes the baseline candidates to **wholesale price columns
regardless of which target is being evaluated**:

```python
CANDS = [("어제 가격", ANCHOR),
         ("최근7일 평균", "whsl_prc_avg7"),      # wholesale, even when target is auction
         ("작년 동시기", "whsl_prc_prev_yr")]    # same
```

Only the first candidate tracks the target. For auction and retail, the other two
compare against a **different price series entirely** — which is why auction radish
showed a "7-day average" baseline of 0.5057 against a target averaging ~554 원/kg.

Consequence: **"strongest baseline = yesterday's price" was true by construction**
for auction and retail. The model was only ever measured against its own anchor.

This is the same class of error as the volume model's "+40% over persistence"
corrected yesterday. Two occurrences in two days, from the same root cause: the
anchor transform makes the anchor *look* like the natural baseline.

Retail is worse off — `rtl_prc_lag1` is the **only** retail price column in
`crop_price_train`. There is nothing correct to compare it against yet.

## 3. The experiment: shrinkage anchor

Anchor replaced with `α · 어제값 + (1−α) · 7일평균`. α = 1.0 is current.
Baselines held fixed across all α, so every row is measured with the same yardstick.

| α | Fold A WMAPE | vs strongest | Fold B WMAPE | vs strongest | summed gain vs α=1.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1.00** | 0.1767 ± 0.0015 | **−3.4%** | 0.1806 ± 0.0010 | +9.8% | — |
| 0.80 | 0.1716 ± 0.0009 | −0.4% | 0.1781 ± 0.0012 | +11.1% | 0.0076 |
| **0.60** | **0.1667 ± 0.0008** | **+2.5%** | **0.1781 ± 0.0014** | **+11.1%** | **0.0125** |
| 0.50 | 0.1646 ± 0.0005 | +3.7% | 0.1788 ± 0.0010 | +10.7% | 0.0139 |
| 0.40 | 0.1632 ± 0.0010 | +4.5% | 0.1797 ± 0.0026 | +10.3% | 0.0144 |
| 0.30 | 0.1624 ± 0.0005 | +5.0% | 0.1808 ± 0.0012 | +9.7% | 0.0141 |
| 0.20 | 0.1620 ± 0.0013 | +5.2% | 0.1819 ± 0.0005 | +9.2% | fold B negative |
| 0.00 | 0.1638 ± 0.0009 | +4.1% | 0.1848 ± 0.0005 | +7.7% | fold B negative |

Adoption threshold: 2 × (0.0015 + 0.0010) = **0.0050**.

**The two folds want different optima** — fold A keeps improving down to α≈0.2, fold B
peaks at α≈0.6–0.8 and degrades below 0.4. Both folds are positive only for α ≥ 0.4.

**Recommend α = 0.6.** It sits at fold B's optimum, clears the threshold by 2.5×
(0.0125 vs 0.0050), and leaves margin in both folds rather than chasing fold A's
tail. α = 0.5 has a marginally larger sum but its fold-B margin (0.0018) is inside
twice its own seed spread.

Seed stability also improves: fold A spread falls from ±0.0015 to ±0.0008.

## 4. Onion may not need shelving after all

Per-item improvement against the corrected baseline:

| α | Fold A 양파 | Fold B 양파 |
|---|---|---|
| 1.00 (current) | **−4.5%** | +3.9% |
| 0.60 | **+4.4%** | **+4.4%** |

Onion auction is our only combination that loses to its baseline, and it has been
attributed to onion price being near a random walk (ACF₁ 0.983). At α = 0.6 it turns
positive in **both** folds.

**The onion problem looks partly like an anchor problem, not a modelling ceiling.**
Worth revisiting the decision to shelve it once α = 0.6 is adopted and re-measured.

## 5. Excluding onion from training — no clear benefit

Tested α = 0.6 with 배추/무 only:

| | 배추 fold A | 배추 fold B | 무 fold A | 무 fold B |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 items | +0.9% | +16.5% | +2.6% | +6.6% |
| 2 items (no onion) | +1.7% | **+10.2%** | +4.2% | +12.6% |

Radish improves in both folds; cabbage improves in A and degrades in B. Signs
disagree for cabbage, so by the two-fold rule this is **판정 불가** — no reason to
drop onion from training. The global model keeps its sample-size advantage.
