## In one sentence

Auction and retail were never given the derived-column family that wholesale has, so
neither can be measured against a proper baseline; build the missing columns, make
the baseline selection target-aware, then re-measure all three targets and decide on
the shrinkage anchor.

## Why it came up

Two findings from earlier today, in order:

1. `train.py` picks its baseline candidates from **wholesale columns regardless of
   target**. For auction, cabbage's target averages 721 원/kg while the wholesale
   7-day average offered as a "candidate" averages 1,169 원/kg — a different series.
   The mismatched candidates always lost on WMAPE, so the anchor was always selected
   and every auction/retail figure is really *"versus the anchor"*.
2. Measured against a proper auction-series baseline, a **plain 7-day moving average
   (0.1709) beats the current auction model (0.1767) in validation 2023**. Shrinking
   the anchor to `0.6·yesterday + 0.4·7-day` turns that into +2.5% and holds +11.1%
   in the other fold.

So the measurement fix and the model fix are one piece of work: without the first,
the second has no yardstick.

## Not a data problem

Auction source data is more complete than wholesale, not less:

| | source coverage | derived columns in `crop_price_train` |
|---|---|---|
| wholesale | 2,857 base dates | **7** — lag1, lag3, lag7, avg7, avg14, std7, prev_yr |
| **auction** | **3,528 trading days** | **3** — lag1, lag3, avg7 |
| retail | present | **1** — lag1 |

Missing rate for what does exist is 0.00% on both `auc_prc_lag1` and `auc_prc_avg7`.

The columns are absent because `23_add_three_targets.sql` added two target columns on
the stated premise that *"the 28 features are completely identical"* across targets.
Under an anchor transform they are not — each target has a paired anchor, the anchor
is the baseline, and the baseline candidate set is therefore target-specific.

**No collection work is needed.** Both source tables already hold everything, and the
daily batch keeps them current. This is running the same window calculations already
written for wholesale against two more series.

## What it changes, and what it does not

**Does not change** the models now in production. The wrong baseline columns were
never labels; nothing needs retraining because of the bug. Wholesale is unaffected
throughout — its baselines were correct.

**Does change** every recorded auction and retail improvement figure, because they
will finally be measured against the right yardstick. Existing records stay as they
are but need the qualifier *"versus the anchor"* attached, which keeps them valid
under the project's own rule that no figure is recorded without its conditions.

**May change** the standing conclusion that onion auction is unfixable. At α = 0.6
onion turns positive in both folds (−4.5% → +4.4% in fold A), which suggests the
onion problem is partly an anchor problem rather than a modelling ceiling.

## One design question that has to be answered first

`lead_biz_d` runs on the **wholesale survey axis**, so the training table carries
2,857 base dates against auction's 3,528 trading days. Auction trades on Saturdays
and some public holidays; the survey does not.

That means roughly **671 auction trading days are currently unused**, and the same
gap already surfaced in yesterday's handoff design as missing Saturday forecasts.

Before writing the new auction columns, decide which axis they are computed on:

- **survey axis** — consistent with everything else, discards Saturday auctions
- **auction axis** — uses all auction days, but then `auc_prc_avg7` spans a different
  set of days than `whsl_prc_avg7`, and the two are features in the same model

I have not resolved this and would not choose it silently. The narrower reading is
that a 7-day auction average should average *auction* days, since that is what the
series is; the risk is a subtle inconsistency between two features that look alike.
