## 2. Two-stage forecasting — predict arrivals, then price

**Proposal:** forecast arrival volume first, feed it into the price model.

**Premise was correct.** Every volume feature in the price model is
backward-looking (`arr_qty_lag1`, `arr_qty_avg7`, `arr_qty_prev_yr`,
`auc_vol_lag1`). The model has *no* information about arrivals during the
forecast horizon — up to 18 business days.

**Result: rejected.** All runs are auction price, 배추/무/양파, train from
2017-01-01, 5 seeds, anchor log-ratio. Fold A = valid 2023, Fold B = valid 2022.
Adoption threshold (2 × summed seed std) = 0.0056.

| Input to the price model | Fold A | Fold B | Gain | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| current, 31 features | 0.1801 ± 0.0017 | 0.1820 ± 0.0011 | — | |
| **oracle** (true future volume) | 0.1749 ± 0.0014 | 0.1747 ± 0.0015 | 0.0125 | ✅ |
| surprise only (true − forecast) | 0.1775 ± 0.0009 | 0.1786 ± 0.0012 | 0.0060 | ✅ |
| **cascade** (causal forecast) | 0.1800 ± 0.0018 | 0.1810 ± 0.0009 | **0.0011** | ✗ |

### Why it fails

The volume model builds its forecast from features the price model **already
has**:

```
arr_qty_lag1 35.0% + arr_qty_prev_yr 17.8% + holiday_remain_d 12.1% + arr_qty_avg7 5.6%
= 70.5% of importance — every one already a price-model input
```

So the forecast is a nonlinear recombination of existing inputs; gradient
boosting can form those itself.

Decomposition proves it. The **surprise** (actual − forecast, std 224 t against
a 530 t mean, |r| ≤ 0.13 against every existing feature) recovers 0.0060 of the
0.0125 oracle gain. The predictable half recovers 0.0011.

**Roughly 5× the value sits in the component that is, by construction,
unforecastable.** That makes the cascade self-defeating rather than merely weak:
a better volume model produces output *closer* to what existing features already
imply, so redundancy grows.

**One real find:** with true future volume, onion auction flips from −1.3% to
**+1.3%**. It is our only combination that loses to the anchor. The signal is
real; a volume model cannot deliver it. Look instead for *already-published*
forward supply information (shipment plans, KREI outlook).

## 3. Routing production-area weather through volume only

**Proposal:** weather acts on price through supply, so give weather to the
volume model alone and drop it from the price model.

| Price model variant | Fold A | Fold B |
|---|---|---|
| current (weather in, 31 feat) | 0.1801 ± 0.0017 | 0.1820 ± 0.0011 |
| weather removed (24 feat) | **0.1791** ± 0.0007 | **0.1906** ± 0.0011 |
| weather removed + volume forecast (25 feat) | 0.1797 ± 0.0008 | 0.1901 ± 0.0010 |

Dropping weather **helps** in 2023 (−0.0010) and **hurts badly** in 2022
(+0.0086, 4× its own threshold). Adding the volume forecast back recovers
0.0005 — noise. **Rejected.**

### The mechanism, in one table

배추, H2 2022. Typhoon Hinnamnor made landfall **6 September 2022**.

| Month | arrivals (t) | idx | price (원/kg) | idx |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jun | 424 | 100 | 805 | 100 |
| Aug | 574 | 135 | 1,237 | 154 |
| **Sep** | **625** | **147** | **2,072** | **257** |
| Nov | 857 | 202 | 476 | 59 |

**In September arrivals rose 47% while price rose 157%.** November shows the
normal inverse relation (volume 202, price 59); September inverts it. The storm
destroyed crop *quality* and shifted scarcity expectations while growers rushed
salvageable stock to market, *raising* short-run arrivals.

Weather reaches price through grade composition and expectations — channels
tonnage cannot carry. During a shock the volume→price mapping flips sign, so a
volume forecast is not a weak proxy for weather; it points the wrong way exactly
when it matters.

## 5. Volume model evaluation — corrected twice

This is the part I got wrong, and it is worth reading as a process note.

### Error 1 — measured persistence, not a model

I first compared volume only to a *persistence* baseline and concluded volume
was harder to forecast than price. The supporting statistics were real:

| Item | Series | CV | ACF(1) | ACF(14) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 배추 | volume | 0.528 | 0.780 | 0.451 |
| | price | 0.441 | 0.736 | 0.376 |
| 양파 | volume | 0.250 | **0.413** | 0.238 |
| | price | 0.351 | **0.983** | 0.814 |

Onion volume is *less volatile* than onion price yet twice as hard for
persistence, because persistence only fails on variance that does not carry
over. But **persistence is not the ceiling.** A trained model changed the
picture entirely.

### Error 2 — compared against the weakest baseline

I then reported "**+40% over persistence**". Arrivals are strongly seasonal, so
**previous-year same period** is a far stronger baseline. Against the strongest
of three, the honest number is **+15%**.

Causal (expanding-window) forecasts, 2023–2026:

| Item | model | prev-year | 7-day avg | yesterday | vs strongest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **all** | **0.1676** | 0.1979 | 0.2246 | 0.2704 | **+15.3%** |
| 배추 | 0.1867 | 0.2276 | 0.2756 | 0.3158 | +18.0% |
| 무 | 0.1621 | 0.1975 | 0.2198 | 0.2697 | +17.9% |
| 양파 | 0.1604 | 0.1801 | 0.1975 | 0.2434 | +10.9% |

### A third slip, caught in passing

An intermediate comparison showed 2023 at 0.2040 while the single-fold run gave
0.1684. Cause: **2.7% of rows had a prediction of 0** (anchor fill where
`arr_qty_lag1 ≤ 0`), each contributing 100% error. Excluding them gives 0.1682 —
matching. Filter invalid predictions before computing any comparison metric.

### What the volume model actually is

| | vs strongest baseline | all three items positive? |
|---|---|---|
| auction price | +5.4% | no (onion −0.4%) |
| wholesale price | +13.3% | no (radish, onion) |
| retail price | +16.0% | yes |
| **arrivals** | **+15.3%** | **yes (+10.9 … +18.0)** |

Comparable to retail — our best price model — and **positive on all three
items**, which none of the price models manage across the board.

The horizon profile is flat:

```
LT1  +13.0%    LT7  +14.8%    LT14 +15.2%    LT18 +14.8%
     0.1668         0.1686         0.1686         0.1678
```

**18 business days out is as accurate as tomorrow.** Price degrades from 0.134
to 0.191 over the same span. Arrivals run on calendar structure, not momentum.

Two caveats:

- **Needs ~4 years of training data.** By year: 2017 −17.9%, 2018 −3.8%, then
  2019 +15.3%, 2020 +13.8%, 2021 +3.5%, 2022 +19.3%, 2023 +8.5%, 2024 +19.7%,
  2025 +19.2%, 2026 +11.1%.
- **Anchor choice does not matter.** Switching the anchor to `arr_qty_prev_yr`
  gave 0.1682 vs 0.1684 — the model already uses it as a feature.

## 6. How volume error compares to price error

Same window, same items, real scored outcomes — `prediction_log` for prices
(`ops_*` models) against causal volume forecasts. 2026-01 to 2026-08,
배추/무/양파.

| Target | WMAPE | mean abs error | mean actual | median % | p80 | p90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **arrivals** | **0.1800** | **109 t** | 606 t | 13.5% | 27.1% | 39.1% |
| auction price | 0.2022 | 144 원/kg | 710 원/kg | 11.9% | 26.2% | 37.1% |
| wholesale price | 0.0933 | 92 원/kg | 981 원/kg | 6.6% | 16.5% | 25.0% |
| retail price | 0.0700 | 189 원/unit | 2,699 원/unit | 5.8% | 11.6% | 15.2% |

**Arrivals sit at almost exactly the difficulty of auction price**, with a
near-identical error distribution (median 13.5% vs 11.9%, p90 39% vs 37%). Both
are raw market signals. Wholesale and retail are more than twice as easy —
distribution margin damps the series.

Concretely: on an average day of 606 t we are off by 109 t. Half the days land
inside 13.5%, but **one day in ten misses by 39% or more** — 600 t expected,
370 t or 830 t delivered. Any warehouse-intake plan has to be designed against
that tail, not the mean.

### Per item

| Item | arrivals | mean t | auction price | mean 원/kg |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 배추 | 0.1991 | 409 | **0.2764** | 816 |
| 무 | 0.1706 | 571 | 0.1685 | 554 |
| 양파 | 0.1771 | 840 | 0.1471 | 760 |

Cabbage arrivals are much easier than cabbage price (0.199 vs 0.276); onion is
the reverse.

### Per lead time — the real difference

| LT | arrivals | auction price |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.1761 | 0.1821 |
| 7 | 0.1810 | 0.1873 |
| 14 | 0.1814 | **0.2224** |
| 18 | 0.1811 | **0.2446** |

**Arrivals at 18 business days are as accurate as tomorrow** (0.176 → 0.181).
Auction price degrades 0.182 → 0.245, a 35% deterioration. The gap widens with
horizon: comparable at LT1, arrivals 26% more accurate at LT18.

For storage trading — which needs a three-week view — that property matters.
Price blurs with distance; supply does not.

## 7. Decisions

1. **Do not build the two-stage cascade for price.** 0.0011 against a 0.0056
   threshold, two folds, causal forecasts.
2. **Keep production-area weather in the price model.** Fold-dependent, and the
   volume forecast does not substitute.
3. **The volume model is worth keeping on its own terms** — backlog `M-16`. But
   it is outside our scope (price forecasting), so ask logistics and purchasing
   whether they want it before building anything.
4. **Always report improvement against the strongest of several baselines.**
   Added to `CLAUDE.md` §11 and saved as a standing rule.
